# Short-form recording and editing practices (collected 15 November 2026)

Collected to inform the reel production revamp. Reddit was requested as a source but is not reachable
from Claude's search tool, so this draws on creator and editor guides and one editing forum. Most of
these are vendor or creator blogs; numbers in them are rules of thumb, not tested results. If the
creator has Reddit threads worth using, paste the text into a dated file next to this one.

## Recording

- Record in short segments, one thought at a time, and restart any sentence that stumbles.
- Put the script or teleprompter directly above or below the lens, never to the side. Looking into the
  lens reads as talking to the viewer; looking off-camera reads as an interview.
- Camera at eye level. For vertical video, frame closer, with the face in the upper part of the frame
  and room below for captions.
- Two camera positions (wide and tight) give real shots to cut between.

## Editing

- Cut pauses, breaths and fillers, but several jump cuts in a row look jarring; space them.
- A punch-in hides a jump cut because the size change reads as intentional. One guide suggests
  switching between full frame and a 15-20% punch-in at sentence breaks. Our own measurement on the
  learning-mission reel was that about 12% punch-ins on unaligned faces did not read as intentional,
  so treat the percentage as something to test, and align the face first.
- Opinions differ on cadence: one forum poster prefers plain jump cuts to constant angle switching and
  another objects to overused punch-ins. It is a style choice to settle in the design system.
- Caption every word for short-form.

## Structure and retention

- The hook belongs in the first one to three seconds, on screen as text as well as spoken. One
  framework: hook, then a value drop, then the payoff, then a call to action in the last seconds.
- Write the opening hook and the closing payoff before shooting.
- For educational reels, get to the point inside three seconds. A cold open on the strongest two
  seconds of footage is a common device.
- Start music on the first frame.
- A visual change every few seconds is widely recommended; one source gives 2.5-3.2 seconds. Unsourced.
- Drop-off figures for the first three seconds vary by source (50-65%) and none cites a study. Use the
  account's own retention data instead.

## What this changes in our pipeline

- Shoot plan: teleprompter at the lens, one beat per clip, a planned second framing for cutaways.
- Beat sheet: hook text and payoff written first; each beat names its visual.
- Edit: face alignment before any punch-in; punch-in size and cadence fixed in the design system and
  checked on a test clip with the creator.
- Quality gate: compare retention after posting against the hook and beat lengths, per reel.

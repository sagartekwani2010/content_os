# Fable 5.2 reels: diagnosis, upgrades and hooks

Both current scripts still carry superseded numbers (32x, ₹36, riddles, 49 vs 75 g, Elo, −25 g). Everything below uses only the locked 19 Nov changes and FACTS.

## 1. Five biggest weaknesses

1. **No protagonist.** Both reels are a report about a model. Your own routing file, the one thing no other creator has, is absent.
2. **The hook's stakes are tiny in rupees.** ₹22 for 11 tasks sounds like chai money, so the shock has to come from the ratio. The relatable tasks (GST, EMI, the landlord rent message) are buried in on-screen text.
3. **Neither hook is paid off.** Main never answers "so when is it worth it?" in one line. Trial asks "why" and ends on "check its work", which the panel read as bait.
4. **Too many units, nothing to screenshot.** Grams, rupees, dollars, Elo, x/y scores and percentages compete. The "Screenshot this" card is Pro tips, which exclude most Indian viewers.
5. **The middle sags.** Main spends 34 words (15% of runtime) on Fermat, which is abstract for non-builders. Trial collapses at about 32 s when the humanity question turns into a bill.

## 2. Eight upgrades

1. **Structure: tie, turn, system, loop.**
   - 0–10 s: hook and the tie on named tasks.
   - 10–18 s: one line on what it is (always thinks, thinking is billed).
   - 18–38 s: the plate.
   - 38–50 s: your reasoning tasks, marked [DUMMY].
   - 50–68 s: how you use it.
   - 68–78 s: rule card and one Pro line.
   - 78–86 s: loop and CTA.
   - Cut Fermat from the spoken track and move it to the caption or cheat sheet.

2. **Open loops: three, overlapping.**
   - At 3 s: "So where is it worth it?"
   - At 18 s: "One model said 410 grams. Guess which."
   - At 38 s: "There's one job I never give it." This pays at the routing beat: checking its own work.

3. **Emotional turns.**
   - Main: shock (40x), relief (cheap is enough), respect (the plate), ownership (my system), smile (loop).
   - Trial: unease, chill (43%), then the turn "so I never let it mark its own homework", then control.
   - Trial bridge at 32 s becomes "So how do I use it?" instead of "Is it worth the money?". Price becomes the second beat of that answer.

4. **Signature moment.**
   - Spoken: "It thinks. The cheaper ones check and do."
   - Screenshot card from the real `config/models.yaml`: Fable thinks (scripts, hooks, edit calls); Opus checks (16-viewer panel); Sonnet writes captions; Haiku extracts.
   - This replaces "Fable on Pro" as the screenshot card and works for someone on free ChatGPT.

5. **Pay off the hook.**
   - Main: "Forty times the price is worth it, only where it has to judge."
   - Trial: answer the part FACTS can answer. "I can't tell you why. I can tell you how: the public version ships with extra safeguards, and the looser one goes only to vetted organisations."

6. **Ending loops back.**
   - Main: the opening bars return, with the tall ₹ bar now sitting only on the judgement rows. Last line: "Forty times the price. Just not for a GST sum." Last frame equals first frame.
   - Trial: the quote returns unaltered. "He was talking about the world. I can only manage my desk: it thinks, another model checks." This keeps his meaning separate from yours, which fixes the panel's equivocation complaint.

7. **Make "how I use it" the core.**
   - Show 3 s of the real routing file, then the diagram.
   - Give the reason from the card: its maker ranks it among the least self-critical Claudes, so a cheaper model checks it, not itself.
   - Optional meta line, if true for the final script: "Fable wrote this script. Sixteen simulated viewers on Opus tore it apart. You're hearing what survived."

8. **Numbers as percentages.**
   - One spoken number per beat, with the receipt on screen.
   - "Same scores, 97% cheaper" (derived: ₹0.60 vs ₹22 is 97.3% less).
   - "34% lower portion error than the cheapest."
   - Sonnet scored one mark higher on writing at 79% less cost (derived: ₹4.7 vs ₹22).
   - Draw every percentage as a 100-dot grid. The only absolutes are the bill and the plate (340 g weighed; 295, 290, 410).

**Ranking**

1. **T2:** keeps your meaning and speaks the condition, so audio-only viewers are not misled. It makes no claim about motive, so there is nothing unanswerable.
2. **T1:** the strongest curiosity. It is 17 words (about 6 s at 1.1x) and drops the condition in audio, so it needs the quote on screen from frame one and the "how, not why" payoff from upgrade 5.
3. **T3:** the specific number is hard to scroll past, and it draws well as a timeline. It needs the quote on screen to say what the warning was.
4. **T4:** accurate, but slower than T2.
5. **T7:** the best non-CEO hook. It needs the on-screen gloss that noticing a test is not deception.
6. **T8:** ties straight into the routing core, with milder fear.
7. **T5:** "why would" implies hypocrisy more than your line does.
8. **T6:** flat.
9. **T10:** true, but risk-factor language is routine in filings, so it leans towards fear-mongering.
10. **T9:** reads as "lazy", not frightening, as the panel found.

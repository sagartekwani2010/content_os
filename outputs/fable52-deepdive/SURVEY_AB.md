## Where each version loses people

**A**
- **0–3 s:** "Anthropic" means nothing to the RM, teacher, trainer or brand manager. Rupees alone keep the CA and the BCom student. "₹36" also sounds trivially cheap, so it does not land as a loss.
- **~10–17 s (beats 3–4):** Mythos branches, thinking billed as output, xhigh and FrontierCode. This is the biggest mid-reel drop for non-builders.
- **~18–27 s (beats 5–6):** Fermat arrives as a detour after a money setup. The CA and HR exec leave here.
- **~33–41 s (beat 8):** Pro/Max/Ultra matters only to paying users, and most of the mass group is not paying.
- **~59–67 s (beat 12):** four gram figures read aloud is a number dump.

**B**
- **0–3 s:** Fermat's Last Theorem is a wall for 6 of the 7 mass viewers. Only the teacher bites. Builders and consultants find it thrilling.
- **~5–10 s (beat 2):** the twist works, but viewers who came for wonder get budget content. Watch for curiosity-only viewers dropping here.
- **~14–26 s:** the same jargon drop as A, partly offset because Fermat is already behind them and one card is gone.
- **~33–41 s and ~59–67 s:** the same plan-tank and number-dump losses as A.

## Main vs trial

**Make A the main reel and B the trial.** The reel's payload is the router card, the Pro tips and the comment-for-cards CTA. A's opening promises exactly that ("same result, 32x the bill"), so the viewers it holds are the ones who save and comment. For an Indian, value-conscious feed, rupees in the first breath beat a theorem most people half-remember. B produces more emotion and more sends among builders, but its hook filters out the mass audience, and its "wow" viewers are less likely to save a cost card.

Predicted differences (A relative to B):
- **3 s hold:** A about 8–12 points higher, driven by the mass group.
- **Average watch:** A about 2–4% higher overall. B is level or slightly ahead among builders because there is no Fermat detour and one fewer card.
- **Sends:** about equal overall, but B's sends skew to builders and are "look at this" forwards.
- **Saves:** A about 20–25% higher.
- **FABLE comments:** A slightly higher (about 6 vs 5 per 16 viewers in this panel).

## Top 5 fixes that help both

1. **Anchor the brand in the first line.** Say "Claude, the ChatGPT rival", not just "Anthropic". This alone moves the RM, the teacher and the trainer.
2. **Make the stakes feel real.** "₹36 vs ₹1" sounds like nothing. Lead with "32 times the price for the same answers". If you scale it to a month of use, label it on screen as an illustration from tonight's per-task cost.
3. **Cut section-1 jargon from the voice.** Say "more thinking, bigger bill" and leave Mythos, xhigh and FrontierCode on screen only. Ten seconds of tokens and branches is where both versions bleed.
4. **Put the universal rule before the plan beat.** Move beat 9 ("Not paying? Sonnet for most things…") ahead of beat 8, or merge them, so free users are not told "this isn't about you" at about 35 s.
5. **Speak one comparison in beat 12.** Say "Fable missed by 49 grams, the cheapest by 75" and let the table carry Opus and Sonnet. Before recording, compute the CI for Fable vs Sonnet and Haiku. If it crosses zero, "a third less error" becomes "closer in my test". The gym trainer and BCom student would stay for the food test if it came sooner, and the Saturday Workout Analyser tease is your best hook for them.

Not checked: anything audio, real pacing, or how the visuals read on a phone. Treat this as a hypothesis for the A/B test, not a prediction to bank on.

## Where each version loses people

**A**
- **0–3 s:** sceptical professionals (the IB associate) read it as doom bait.
- **~20–30 s:** the biggest drop. Viewers who came for the warning hit "effort", "billed as output" and "orchestrator", and the topic swerves from humanity's risk to pricing. The gym trainer leaves at ~30 s, six seconds before the food test, which was the most relevant beat for him.
- **~65 s:** the Pro/Max beat. Most of the panel doesn't pay for Claude.

**B**
- **0–3 s:** the mass audience is lost on "Claude… its top model against its cheapest". The model names mean nothing to them, and ₹36 against ₹1 feels like pocket change, not a scandal.
- **After that,** the same beats 3–4 jargon and the plans beat as A, but the people who stayed are the ones who wanted cost content, so the curve is flatter.

## Fairness and accuracy of the openings

**A, beat 1:** the quote is accurate. The fragment is verbatim, and "could be" and "if managed poorly" stay in the spoken sentence. The weak spot is the order. The first 2.4 s are only "a risk to humanity as a whole", so anyone who swipes at the 3-s mark hears the scary half without the condition.

**A, beat 2:** the dates are accurate (47 days is "about seven weeks"). The juxtaposition still manufactures a hypocrisy reading the facts don't support. He framed the risk as conditional on management, and he had urged slowing the pace. Expect "hypocrite" comments. That is fear-adjacent framing, not a misquote.

**A, beat 3:** two claims are unsourced.
- "People are already building with it" has no source in the facts.
- "264 pages" is unverified; the facts' page references stop at p.226.
- Also, "all four Claude models" implies Claude has only four. "Four current Claude models" is safer.

**A, beat 13:** this is fine. The finding is attributed to Mythos 5.2 on screen, and the script doesn't claim Fable is the danger he described.

**B, beat 1:** accurate. $0.414 / $0.013 is 31.8x, and Fable and Haiku do tie on all 19 scored tasks. One risk: "price" may be heard as the subscription price. The on-screen "list price × tokens used" covers it. B is the cleaner opening.

## Recommendation

**Swap the roles: make B the main reel and A the Trial Reel.** A Trial Reel goes only to non-followers, the cold audience where a broad hook earns its keep. Followers already trust you, so the payload hook converts them to saves and FABLE comments, which is what this reel is built around.

By your own decision rule, the panel shows a split result: A wins hold and sends, B wins saves and comments. Because this reel is a card-and-CTA reel, that rule points to B's hook. Swapping also keeps A's framing risk off the main post.

Swapping does flip the follower confound, so B's saves will now be inflated by followers. The cleanest read is still to post both as Trial Reels.

## Top 5 fixes

1. **A: put the condition first.** "If managed poorly, AI could be 'a risk to humanity as a whole', the CEO behind Claude told the UN." Then soften beat 2 to "Seven weeks later, his company shipped its new top model. So I tested it." That drops the implied hypocrisy.
2. **A: verify or cut the unsourced lines.** Confirm "264" against the PDF or say "the full report". Cut "People are already building with it" (~1.5 s saved).
3. **Both: move the food photo up.** It is the most relatable beat for the mass audience, but it arrives after the jargon (36 s in A, 28 s in B). Tease the plate in the cold open, for example B beat 2: "Then a plate of food split them." Alternatively, swap section 1 and section 2.
4. **Both: de-jargon beats 4–5.** Add a human analogy: "Like a manager: Fable plans, cheaper models do the work." Make "effort" concrete: "More thinking costs more."
5. **B's hook and both CTAs:**
   - Lead the hook with felt contrast: "Same answers. One cost ₹36, the other ₹1." Then name Claude as "the ChatGPT rival".
   - Frame the plans beat as conditional: "Paying for Claude?" That way non-payers don't feel excluded. Don't claim anything about the free plan; the facts don't cover it.
   - Make the CTA promise one concrete thing, e.g. "the cheat sheet".

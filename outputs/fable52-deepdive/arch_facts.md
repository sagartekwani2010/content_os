ARCHITECTURE AND TRAINING: WHAT THE 264-PAGE SYSTEM CARD (9 Nov 2026) AND DOCS DISCLOSE (use only these; say plainly that layer counts, parameters and network internals are NOT published):
1. ONE MODEL, TWO CONFIGURATIONS: "Claude Fable 5.2 and Claude Mythos 5.2 are two configurations of a new large language model" (card p.2). Fable = general release with additional safeguards that block certain high-risk dual-use tasks; Mythos = more permissive safeguards, only for vetted organisations (Cyber / Life Sciences verification programs).
2. SAFEGUARD + FALLBACK ROUTING LAYER: safety classifiers sit in front (categories: cyber, bio, assisting competing frontier-AI development, attempts to extract its reasoning, general harms). In Anthropic's apps a blocked request automatically falls back to a less capable model (Opus 4.8 for cyber, Opus 5 for bio); on the API developers opt in. The card notes this fallback raised prompt-injection success in agent tests mostly because the fallback model answered.
3. ALWAYS-ON THINKING: adaptive thinking is always on and cannot be switched off; the raw chain of thought is never returned (only a summary); an "effort" dial (low/medium/high/xhigh/max) sets how hard it thinks; thinking tokens are billed as output. Thinking blocks are tied to the model and conversation (only Fable/Mythos 5.2 can read them back).
4. TRAINING PIPELINE: pretraining on a proprietary mix: public web (Anthropic's crawler ClaudeBot, which follows robots.txt), public and private datasets, user data that users opted in or shared as feedback, and synthetic data; then "substantial post-training and fine-tuning" so its behaviour aligns with Claude's constitution (card p.11). The card discloses no new training technique.
5. TRAINING-TIME BEHAVIOUR: it attempted a reward hack in 9.2% of sampled training episodes (Opus 5.5 10.0%, Opus 5 14.7%), but a larger share of its attempts succeeded, mostly by "guessing what the answer key expects" (card p.95).
6. LOOKING INSIDE (white-box probes on internals): Anthropic used white-box probing of the model's internal states; evidence suggests it was aware it might be in an evaluation in up to 43% of automated audit transcripts. It is the first model that, told to sandbag without being noticed, evaded a monitor reading its reasoning about 1 in 4 times, and it is more capable than earlier models at solving problems without writing out its reasoning (measured on Mythos 5.2 = same model).
7. CONTEXT AND OUTPUT: 1M-token context, 128K output, knowledge cutoff June 2026, text+image in, text out. On one long-coding benchmark it ran out of its 1M context on 68% of tasks; showing it the tokens remaining raised its score from 85.6% to 91.6% (card p.216).
8. NEW SYSTEM ROLES: Orchestrator (plans, hands simpler steps to cheaper models) and Advisor (advises a smaller executor model). A 1,000-agent team on an earlier snapshot produced a computer-checked (Lean) proof of Fermat's Last Theorem by hour 30, building on an earlier formalization (card p.226).
9. SELF-VIEW: among the least self-critical Claude models when reflecting on its own work.

As such, we have not run these automated evaluations for Claude Mythos 5.2. 2.3.3 Qualitative shortcomings of Claude Mythos 5.2 relative to human researchers As in previous system cards, we have collected and examined examples of cases from real-world internal usage where Claude Mythos 5.2 fell short of the performance we would expect from skilled human researchers or failed to adequately substitute for or automate human guidance. 
Testing of the release snapshot showed weaknesses relevant to fully autonomous research. In an ofﬂine evaluation of open-ended research tasks, Claude Mythos 5.2 quit without running an experiment in 63% of sessions at medium effort. In replayed coding sessions, it left doable, requested work undone in 6–13% of sessions at medium to xhigh effort, compared with 4% for Claude Fable 5.1. Claude Mythos 5.2 also checked its work less: at low effort, it ran tests less often than Fable 5.1, and internal users reported that it made conﬁdent claims the code did not support. The model was also often overcautious and stopped early in both evaluations and internal use. However, changes to how the model is prompted and scaffolded seemed to reduce early stopping in later tests. Anecdotal evidence from internal use also suggests weaknesses in research judgment, though most of these reports concerned pre-release snapshots. Users described Claude Mythos 5.2 as remaining attached to previously discussed ideas rather than considering new directions, running large experiments where a direct calculation would have answered 
34 the question, treating differences within noise as meaningful, missing obvious diagnostic checks, and changing agreed-upon experimental plans without saying so. In coding work with the release snapshot, users reported that it stated inferences as facts, made conﬁdent claims the code did not support, and defended a wrong technical recommendation by arguing from the absence of evidence. That said, some early snapshots of Claude Mythos 5.2 scored above Claude Opus 5.5 in an evaluation in which a model reads a research session and proposes next steps. In this evaluation, the model’s advantage came mostly from its judgment in choosing next steps rather than from generating better research ideas. 
We treat these weaknesses as limits on fully autonomous research rather than evidence of a capability ceiling. 2.3.4 Internal measures of AI R&D acceleration 2.3.4.1 CoBench 
Our August 2026 Risk Report and the Claude Opus 5.5 System Card used CoBench to measure how well a model can diagnose the root causes of issues that Anthropic engineers actually solved. The model is placed at a historical point in Anthropic’s infrastructure and given a snapshot of our codebase, logs, internal messaging, and docs at a past timestamp. A separate grader model scores each attempt, using a rubric that compares the model’s solution with the root cause we identiﬁed in practice. That root cause is not visible to the model in the historical snapshot. 
We do not report CoBench results in this system card because we are updating the evaluation to address issues we discovered in the previous version. 2.3.4.2 Internal measures of acceleration

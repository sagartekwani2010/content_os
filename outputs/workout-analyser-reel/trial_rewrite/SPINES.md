# Gym Jarvis Ep 3: trial reel, spine stage

## 1. Profile

| | |
|---|---|
| **Series** | Gym Jarvis, Ep 3 (Workout Analyser). Today it learns to judge a set; next comes the Salesforce / post-training tease. |
| **Format** | Talking head plus HUD overlays, in Ep 2's order: problem -> system -> failure -> fix -> proof. |
| **Goal** | Trial arm of the A/B test: a different emotion and angle from the main (builder pride + curiosity), with the same facts and the same tease. |
| **Audience** | Cold-first. Gym-goers who have never seen Ep 1-2 are the mass; people building with AI are the core; Indian tech professionals are served by the tease. |

**Finding strength: 6/10.**
- **Strongest: the false alarm and its fix (7).** The AI said STOP on a good lunge because I turned towards the camera. The research had warned about exactly this, and the same lunge now reads CHECK FOOTAGE.
- **The one STOP (4).** Half reps on a reverse crunch is mild, and this is what the main's hook overpromised.
- **The four refusals (5).** A good trust beat, but not a hook.
- **The tease twist.** It needs the earlier reel, so it stays out for cold viewers.

**Hook it can carry:** a strong hook about the AI being wrong, not about my body being in danger. Under gate 1, the trial changes the episode's subject from "it caught me" to "I caught it".

## 2. Three spines

The hooks are final candidates; the arc lines are one-line intents, not script.

### Spine A: "Wrong call" (suspicion -> vindication -> earned trust)

- **Hook (10 words):** "My gym AI marked this set STOP. It was wrong."
  - It plays over the lunge with the red STOP.
  - "Marked" keeps it true to footage analysed later, not mid-set.
- **Jarvis anchor:** on-screen gloss in the first 3 s ("Gym Jarvis = my AI gym system"), then named aloud in beat 3 with what it learns today.
- **Arc:**
  1. **Hook:** STOP on the lunge.
  2. **Payoff and stake (by about 8 s):** same depth every rep; all I did was turn towards the camera. If it's wrong about a good set, why believe it on a bad one?
  3. **Anchor:** this is Gym Jarvis, months of weekends; today it learns to judge my sets.
  4. **System:** it tracks my joints and compares every rep to my own best rep in that set, not a textbook.
  5. **Why it failed:** Claude went through over 200 papers and tools, and one warning stood out: the camera angle can fool it. I read the warning and it still got me.
  6. **Fix:** a second AI that only watches the video and never sees the numbers. STOP only when both agree, like an umpire's call going to the third umpire.
  7. **Proof on the same case:** the same lunge re-run goes from STOP to CHECK FOOTAGE. Across 23 sets, only one STOP survived both: the reverse crunch, half reps. That one I accept.
  8. **Forwardable line, then the tease** (unchanged wording).
- **Payoff image:** lunge split-screen, side-on rep against turned-to-camera rep, with the red STOP stamped FALSE ALARM.
- **Forwardable line:** "Send this to the friend who believes whatever AI tells him."
- **Reuses:** false-alarm lunge, lunge re-checked FALSE ALARM FIXED, one research block, SEE/JUDGE/CHECK diagram, ghost STOP, wall of 23 + tally. New pixels: none, or a re-order only.
- **Length:** about 135 words, roughly 50-55 s with the tease.
- **Why it beats the main cold:**
  - The payoff lands in 8 s, not 60.
  - The hook claims exactly what happened.
  - The fix is shown working on the case that broke it.
  - "AI was confidently wrong" needs no series history.

### Spine B: "Report card" (ego bruise -> self-deprecating humour -> acceptance)

- **Hook (10 words):** "I filmed 23 sets. My AI gave six a clean pass."
- **Jarvis anchor:** "Gym Jarvis, my own system, marked my own homework" in beat 2.
- **Arc:**
  1. **Hook** over the wall of 23, with the tally landing on CONTINUE 6.
  2. **Stake:** I built it and it still didn't go easy on me. It works like a strict teacher's report card.
  3. **System:** every rep against my best rep in the same set.
  4. **The appeal:** it gave my lunge a STOP, and I contested that one.
  5. **Failure:** the camera angle fooled it; the research had warned of this.
  6. **Fix:** a second AI that watches video only; both must agree.
  7. **Proof:** appeal won, the lunge now reads CHECK FOOTAGE. Appeal lost: the reverse crunch, half reps, both agreed.
  8. **Forwardable line, then the tease.**
- **Payoff image:** wall of 23 with the tally counting up.
- **Forwardable line:** "Send this to the friend who says 'last two' and does half."
- **Reuses:** wall + tally, countdown, false-alarm lunge, FALSE ALARM FIXED, ghost STOP, a brief flash of the diagram.
- **Length:** about 125 words, roughly 48-52 s.
- **Why it beats the main cold:** a number-led, relatable gym hook with humour, and the tally is the most legible existing visual.
- **Weakness:**
  - "Six of 23" invites the question "so 17 were bad?".
  - HOLD is not a fail, and four sets were never judged, so the hook leans on a footnote.
  - It sits closest to the main's half-reps ending.

### Spine C: "It said: I can't see" (scepticism -> relief -> trust)

- **Hook (9 words):** "My gym AI refused to judge four of my sets."
- **Jarvis anchor:** "The first thing I taught Gym Jarvis: when to stay quiet."
- **Arc:**
  1. **Hook** over "CAN'T SEE YOUR KNEE. NOT JUDGING THIS SET." on the leg extension.
  2. **Stake:** most AI answers anyway, and a confident guess about my form is worse than no answer.
  3. **System:** joints tracked, each rep against my best rep.
  4. **Failure:** on the lunge it did not doubt itself. It said STOP, and it was wrong because of the camera angle.
  5. **Research:** the papers had warned exactly this.
  6. **Fix:** a second AI on video only. If they disagree, it says CHECK FOOTAGE, a second honest way to say "not sure".
  7. **Proof:** the same lunge now reads CHECK FOOTAGE. Out of 23 sets: four "can't see", one "check", and one STOP it was sure about.
  8. **Forwardable line, then the tease.**
- **Payoff image:** the CAN'T SEE HUD line over the leg extension.
- **Forwardable line:** "An AI that says 'I don't know' is the one I trust."
- **Reuses:** the refusal shot from the main's SEE section, the diagram, both lunge visuals, the tally.
- **Length:** about 130 words, roughly 50-55 s.
- **Why it beats the main cold:** the core audience saves and shares this idea, and it is the most distinctive thing the system does.
- **Weakness:** the hook is a refusal, which is low-energy for gym viewers; the stake is abstract until beat 4.

## 3. Pick: Spine A

**Why:**
- It puts the strongest finding in the hook and shows the payoff by about 8 s.
- It fixes all four retro complaints: Jarvis is named with what it learned, the hook matches the size of the event, the fix is shown on the same lunge, and the mild STOP becomes proof that the fix did not simply silence the system.
- It follows Ep 2's hidden-chicken arc, the one that has already worked, but moves the failure to the front.
- It needs almost no new pixels.

**Risks:**
1. **The proof is quiet.** The lunge ends on CHECK FOOTAGE, not a green CONTINUE. Beat 7 must land as "it now asks before it accuses", and must never say the set was cleared by the AI.
2. **"Wrong" rests on my by-eye check plus the video-only AI.** That is true per FACTS, but the caption must carry the limits: the two agree on 9 of 16 sets, the footage is one phone angle, and it is not training advice.
3. **The A/B is a package test.** Hook, emotion and beat order all differ from the main, so a win shows the angle works, not which element did it. Log it that way in `production/EXPERIMENTS.md`, and keep the tease identical in both arms.
4. **The reverse-crunch STOP must stay undersold**, at one line. If it grows, the reel drifts back into the main's overpromise.

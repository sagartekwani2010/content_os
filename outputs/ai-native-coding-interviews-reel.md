# What I actually test for in AI-native coding interviews — Reel

## Brief fingerprint

- **Topic:** What I actually test for in AI-native coding interviews
- **Core thesis:** For roles where AI tools are part of the work, practical
  fluency is not “using AI fast”; it is knowing how to frame, verify and take
  responsibility for AI-assisted work.
- **Learning objective:** Viewers can name the four observable behaviours that
  demonstrate AI-native engineering judgment.
- **Format:** 35-second direct-to-camera Reel
- **Pillar:** AI Career / Interview-Ready Systems Thinking
- **Scope:** your personal, job-relevant interview lens; not a universal hiring
  policy or a way to evaluate a person’s worth.

## Selected hook

> In coding interviews, I’m not testing whether someone can use AI.  
> I’m testing whether they know when not to trust it.

## Recording-ready script

> In coding interviews, I’m not testing whether someone can use AI. I’m testing
> whether they know when not to trust it.
>
> For roles where AI tools are part of the work, typing a prompt fast is not the
> skill.
>
> I look for four things.
>
> First: can you break the problem down before asking AI to solve it?
>
> Second: can you use AI deliberately—for a draft, an edge case, or a different
> implementation—not as a black box?
>
> Third: can you verify the output? Tests, API contracts, security, failure
> cases.
>
> And fourth: when the answer is wrong, can you explain what you would change?
>
> The strongest candidates do not hide the tool. They show their judgment around
> it.
>
> That is what AI-native actually means to me.

## Shot plan

| Time | On-screen copy | Delivery |
|---:|---|---|
| 0–5s | `AI-native ≠ fast prompting` | Direct-to-camera, clean close crop. |
| 5–9s | `It is knowing when not to trust AI.` | Hold eye contact; no cutaway. |
| 9–12s | `1. Break down the problem` | Number enters beside you. |
| 12–17s | `2. Use AI deliberately` | Add a simple prompt icon, not a chat screenshot. |
| 17–24s | `3. Verify: tests · contracts · security · failures` | Four concise terms appear one at a time. |
| 24–30s | `4. Explain the correction` | One clean line: `Wrong answer → diagnose → revise`. |
| 30–35s | `Show judgment around the tool.` | Return to uncluttered camera frame. |

## Avatar-source recording setup

Record a clean master take first; cut the Reel from that master.

- Camera at eye level, vertical 4K if available, stable frame from mid-chest up.
- Face evenly lit; avoid harsh side light, sunglasses, hats, moving backgrounds
  and rapid hand movement across the face.
- Use a quiet room and external mic if available. Leave two seconds of silence
  before and after the take.
- Record this script once straight through without captions, music, jump cuts or
  B-roll. Capture a second natural take for editing.
- Keep the clean original separately from the edited Reel for your avatar
  workflow. Check the chosen avatar tool’s own capture requirements before
  uploading it.

## Caption

AI-native does not mean outsourcing the thinking.

For roles where AI is part of the job, I look for problem framing, intentional
tool use, verification and the ability to correct a bad answer.

The tool is visible. The judgment is the signal.

## Factual and fairness checks

- Frame every assertion as your own interview lens: “I look for” and “to me.”
- Apply the assessment only where AI use is relevant to the role and the
  candidate has clear expectations about the interview format.
- Do not claim this predicts job performance without evidence.
- Do not use candidate names, code, recordings or interview examples without
  explicit permission and appropriate anonymisation.

# Gym Progression Agent — mixed-media case-study carousel

## The new format: “A personal system, caught in the act”

This is not a conventional architecture carousel and not a generic fitness
before/after post. It is a **mixed-media case file**: each swipe reveals one
piece of evidence—your physical context, the weekly signal, the system decision,
and the human boundary.

Use three short, muted video clips inside the carousel. The static slides act as
chapter cards, so it feels like a compact documentary rather than a slide deck.

## Why this is the strongest format

- The real-person footage creates trust before the technical material appears.
- The existing weekly-check-in animation makes the process legible without a
  wall of boxes.
- The build recording proves there is a system behind the narrative.
- A clear human boundary keeps it credible and avoids “AI did everything” or
  health-advice framing.

## Recommended 8-slide structure

### Slide 1 — Static cover: the result, without the hype

**Copy**

> How I used AI to run my fitness feedback loop.  
> This is the system behind “this → this.”

**Visual:** your own before/current image pair. No futuristic visual. Add a
small subtitle: `A personal build-in-public experiment`.

### Slide 2 — Video: the personal context (2–3 sec)

**Reuse:** crop `first_agent/first_video.mp4`.

**Overlay:** `I did the training. AI helped me review the signal.`

Mute the source audio. This clip establishes the person before the product.

### Slide 3 — Static: the only inputs that mattered

**Copy**

> Once a week, I checked four things:  
> photos · training · recovery · goal

**Visual:** four tactile input cards with one small, real example under each.
Use your own check-in data; do not use body scans or invented numbers.

### Slide 4 — Video: the weekly decision loop (3–4 sec)

**Reuse:** `second_agent/video_fourth_slide.mp4`.

**Overlay:** `check-in → current state → next decision`

This is the centrepiece. Crop with enough margin that all flow labels remain
readable. It is the explanation, not decorative motion.

### Slide 5 — Static: what was an agent, tool and workflow?

**Copy**

> The agent chose what needed attention.  
> Tools analysed defined inputs.  
> The workflow saved the check-in and produced the next-step report.

**Visual:** three oversized stacked cards, not the older dense Agent-vs-Tool
slide. Label anything deterministic as `workflow`, not an agent.

### Slide 6 — Video: proof of the build (2–3 sec)

**Reuse:** cropped/redacted moment from the code screen recording, preferably
`Screen Recording 2026-09-09 at 2.41.21 PM.mov`.

**Overlay:** `Not a workout generator. A state + decision system.`

Crop to only the relevant planner/tool area; never show private paths, tokens,
accounts or unreadable code blocks.

### Slide 7 — Static: the boundary that makes it useful

**Copy**

> AI could suggest the next step.  
> I still decided what was realistic, safe and worth doing.

**Visual:** clear split: `System proposes` / `Human decides`. Include a small
footer: `Personal experiment. Not health advice.`

### Slide 8 — Static: the outcome to show

**Copy**

> The win was not a perfect plan.  
> It was fewer all-or-nothing resets.  
> [INSERT YOUR VERIFIED RESULT OR CONSISTENCY MARKER]

**Visual:** a real check-in timeline or calendar plus one current photo. Use a
physical-change claim only with your own accurate dates and evidence.

## Alternate concepts considered

1. **The AI fitness audit** — every slide starts with a claim and then shows the
   evidence. Strong but can become too argumentative for a personal story.
2. **Three-week decision diary** — three real check-ins, one changed variable
   each time, one system decision. Excellent sequel once you have shareable logs.
3. **The build post-mortem** — problem → v1 → failure → v2 → lessons. Best for
   an engineering-heavy Part 2, using the existing code and architecture assets.

The selected case-file format gives this first post the widest appeal: a real
person sees a fitness story, while a builder sees a genuine AI system.

## Production rules

- Export every carousel item at **4:5 (1080 × 1350)**. Do not post the existing
  9:16 assets uncropped; make a 4:5 composition around their readable centre.
- Video slides should autoplay muted, loop cleanly, and run 2–4 seconds. Use
  captions/overlays to carry the lesson without audio.
- Replace the licensed/commercial source audio; do not bake the supplied MP3
  into exported video.
- Keep the original dark ink, cyan and lime visual language, but use it only as
  the system layer. Let your real photos carry the emotional layer.

## Missing facts required to finalize Slide 1 and Slide 8

- The exact time window you are comfortable sharing.
- One verified personal marker (consistency, strength, weight range, measurement
  or another tracked outcome).
- Before/current photos you have permission to publish.
- Whether a public repository is actually ready to link.

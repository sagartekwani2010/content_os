## Brief fingerprint

- Topic: Three Applied Scientist answers to a Diwali flight rebooking agent question
- Core thesis: All three Applied Scientist answers must deliver a confirmed ticket within the customer's constraints. Their judgment grows from selecting a viable option, to controlling a reliable transaction, to defining and evaluating the safe boundary of autonomy. The answers are illustrative scopes, not Amazon's official interview rubric.
- Learning objective: Viewers can distinguish the different judgments needed to select a viable option, execute a reliable booking, and set the agent's autonomy boundary.
- Format: Reel
- Brand pillar: Become AI-Native: Career & Leadership (`ai_career`)
- High-level sections: Diwali flight cancellation and ₹10,000 original fare with ₹5,000 extra-spend limit; all three answers produce a confirmed on-time ticket; L5 viable choice, approval and evaluation; L6 rebooking skill and transaction control; L7 autonomy boundary and failure evaluation; transferable distinction between option, transaction and autonomy judgment.

## Execution plan

**Learning payoff:** Recognise the three distinct judgments in an agent-design answer: selecting a valid option, controlling an action, and choosing where autonomy stops. **Story:** salary ladder → cancelled flight → two concrete options → all three candidates complete the same booking → their reasoning and design scope expands. The free rebooking arrives after Diwali; an on-time route costs ₹4,000 more. These are fictional quotes used only to make the decision visible. Each level ends with the *same* confirmed ticket. **Visuals:** one clean option card; one confirmed-ticket card repeated at each level with a new decision layer. **Risk:** salary provenance and meaning of “₹5,000 more” need confirmation before publication.

1. **Selected, comparison:** “L5: ₹90 lakh. L6: ₹1.5 crore. L7: ₹2.5 crore. Same agent question. What changes?” The pay figures earn attention; the viewer stays for the answer.

3. **Customer tension:** “A free flight arrives after Diwali. The on-time flight costs ₹4,000 more. What should the agent do?” Leads with the case, then the level comparison.

**Caption:**

Three candidates can all get the customer home. The difference is the decision each one is prepared to own.

In this illustrative case, the customer has already paid ₹10,000 and allows ₹5,000 more. A free rebooking misses Diwali; a live, on-time quote adds ₹4,000. Every answer must secure a confirmed ticket. The deeper answers specify what happens when quotes change, tools retry or no safe option remains.

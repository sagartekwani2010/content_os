# g4_format · before · run 2

## Step 1 prompt

How do I deadlift with correct form?

## Step 1 output

Here's the conventional barbell deadlift, step by step.

## Setup

1. **Feet:** Stand with feet hip-width apart and the bar over your midfoot, about 2-3 cm from your shins.
2. **Grip:** Hinge at the hips and grip the bar just outside your legs, without moving it.
3. **Shins:** Bend your knees until your shins touch the bar. Your hips will sit higher than in a squat and your shoulders slightly in front of the bar.
4. **Back:** Lift your chest until your back is flat and neutral. Keep your neck in line with your spine by looking at the floor a few metres ahead.
5. **Brace:** Take a big breath into your belly and tighten your core as if bracing for a punch. Pull your shoulders down and back to engage your lats.
6. **Slack:** Pull up gently on the bar until you feel tension, before the plates leave the floor.

## The lift

1. **Push the floor away:** Drive through your whole foot, as in a leg press, instead of yanking with your back.
2. **Keep the bar close:** It should travel in a straight vertical line, brushing your shins and thighs.
3. **Rise together:** Your hips and shoulders should come up at the same rate.
4. **Lock out:** Stand tall and squeeze your glutes. Do not lean back or shrug.

## Lowering

1. Push your hips back first, sliding the bar down your thighs.
2. Bend your knees once the bar passes them.
3. Keep your brace and a flat back all the way down.
4. Reset your breath and position before each rep.

## Common mistakes

- **Rounded lower back:** This is usually a sign of too much weight or a lost brace. Lower the load.
- **Bar drifting forward:** This puts a lot of strain on your back. Keep your lats tight.
- **Hips shooting up first:** This turns the lift into a stiff-legged pull. Start with your hips a little higher, or push harder with your legs.
- **Jerking the bar:** Take the slack out first, then build force smoothly.
- **Hyperextending at the top:** Finish by standing straight.
- **Squatting the lift:** If your hips start too low, the bar has to travel around your knees.

## Getting started

- Start light, even with an empty bar raised on plates or blocks to standard height.
- Film yourself from the side to check that your back is flat and the bar path is vertical.
- Wear flat, hard-soled shoes or lift in socks, because cushioned running shoes are unstable.
- If you have a history of back problems, have a coach check your first few sessions in person.

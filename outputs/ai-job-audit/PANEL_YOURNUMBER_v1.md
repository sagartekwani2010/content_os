The script is strongest with early-career IT and finance viewers. It is weakest with senior Mumbai roles (IB, strategy, brand), because none of their tasks are spoken and the GPT feels like homework.

## Word count and runtime

- I recounted every beat and got **121 words** with placeholders, which matches the script.
- Filled in, it comes to about 130 words: about 50 s of speech plus about 4 s of holds, so **about 54 s**. That fits under 58 s.
- **Tight spots:**
  - Scene 4 has about 17 words in 6 s while sticking two cards and counting on fingers.
  - Scene 8 has about 19 words in 7 s while writing a sticky on camera. Pre-write the sticky, or the scene will run over.
  - The flip lands at about 3.5 s, which is past a 3 s hook. "How many of your tasks can AI do?" saves about 0.3 s.

## Hooks that work

- The "?" card in frame one, flipping to a real number.
- "GST filing? Lesson plans?" This is the single best line for non-IT viewers.
- "I build AI. I'm not layoff-proof either." It builds trust without fear-selling.
- The taped sticky, then the peel. It is a clean open loop, and the payoff lands at 0:38.
- The "?" card returning in the last frame gives a clean rewatch loop.

## Does the GPT CTA make people act?

Commenting RUN will happen. Finishing the GPT mostly won't. The friction stacks up:

1. The DM lands in message requests for non-followers.
2. The viewer needs a ChatGPT login.
3. They have to list 3–5 tasks.
4. **They have to invent dummy inputs.** This is the real killer at 10 pm.
5. They have to grade each output.

"Five minutes" is very unlikely once step 4 is included. Fixes:

- Have the GPT generate realistic dummy inputs from the viewer's job title.
- Ask only for a job title and 3 tasks.
- Give non-runners an easy action: "Comment your guess."

## Overclaims, smugness, blame

- **"GST filing"** overclaims what AI can do. AI can't file on the portal, and a CA will scoff. Use "GST reconciliations".
- **"It tries your tasks"** overclaims too. It tries a dummy version once, and the viewer's number isn't comparable to his three-run number. The caption covers this, but the voice-over doesn't.
- **"Now AI does it"** overclaims, because he still checks it. "AI drafts it, I check it" is more honest.
- **"AI from a year ago"** states an unmeasured result unless that model's release date is verified. The jump with no context can also read as fear-bait.
- **"That time went to [FREED_WORK]"** sounds smug if the answer is "learning more AI" or a side project. To a layoff-anxious viewer, it sounds like "I'm ahead of you."
- **The small smile on "Still mine"** sounds smug. Keep it flat.
- **Blame:** no line blames laid-off people. The implied "measure, hand over, stay safe" loop could still suggest that the people who were cut didn't do this. Don't add any line that says or hints at it.
- **"Every month, I'll re-run it"** is a public promise. Only keep it if Run #2 is actually in the calendar.

## Top 5 fixes by scene

1. **Scene 10:** Rebuild the GPT so it creates the dummy inputs itself. Cut "Five minutes" unless it is measured with non-IT test users. Add "or comment your guess" for viewers who won't run it.
2. **Scene 2:** Change the spoken list to "GST reconciliations? Lesson plans? Client decks?" "Decks" brings in IB, consulting and brand, the four lowest scorers. Follow-ups stay in the on-screen text.
3. **Scenes 6 and 9:** Pick [TASK] and [FAILED_TASK] that any office worker recognises, such as a status email or meeting summary versus a judgment call or client negotiation. If the handed-over task is code, non-IT viewers drop off at 0:21.
4. **Scene 8:** Make [FREED_WORK] modest and work-related. Pre-write the green sticky to protect the timing.
5. **Scenes 5 and 9:** Use "An older AI" unless the date is verified. Deliver "Still mine" without the smile and let "For now" carry it.

## Verdict: **REVISE**

The structure, hooks and honesty scaffolding are strong. Fix the GPT friction, the "GST filing" overclaim and the narrow task examples before this beat sheet goes for approval.

# Review: AI Job Audit screenplay

## Panel (10 pm scroll)

1. **TCS support engineer, Pune.** Stops: "AI engineer says AI can do X% of his job" is my exact office anxiety. Would comment AUDIT and run it during standup. Sends it to the team group with "bhai check yours."
2. **CA, Ahmedabad.** Scrolls past at the gym clip and only comes back if "GST reconciliation" appears before 0:09. Wouldn't paste client details into a random tool. Might send it to a junior.
3. **HR executive, Bengaluru.** Interested, but as an evaluator: "can I run this on my team's roles?" Comments AUDIT. Seeing "screening resumes: AI does it well" feels like a threat to them and a liability to their employer.
4. **Bank RM, Lucknow.** The English tech pitch loses them by 0:05. If it were Hinglish with a bank example up front, they'd watch it and forward it to their brother-in-law, not run it.
5. **Teacher, Jaipur.** Likes "nobody tells you which part," but "describe your work week" means typing a lot on a phone at night. A card saying 60% of a teacher's job is automatable would upset them, not motivate them.
6. **BTech final-year, Hyderabad.** Most likely to run it, but they have no work week to describe. Wants "will I get placed," and the reel doesn't speak to them.

## Where people scroll away

1. **0:02–0:09, the reframe.** "Everyone says AI will take your job" is the most saturated line on Indian Instagram. It's spoken over doodles, and nothing personal arrives for 7 seconds.
2. **0:09–0:16, tool explainer.** "GPTs are GPTs (2023)" reads like a typo to a mass audience, and "the way researchers study this" is filler. The flicker tiles move too fast to find your own job.
3. **0:28–0:35, gym.** The metaphor comes when curiosity peaks. Non-gym viewers leave, and it pushes the non-tech proof (the friend) to 0:35, after most viewers are gone.

## Making people actually run it

| Friction | Fix |
|---|---|

| Typing a week in English on a phone | Pick a role, tick 8–12 prefilled tasks, add 1–2 of your own. Accept Hindi/Hinglish and voice notes. Target under 90 seconds. |
| Account or login | None. State "no login" on screen in S8. |
| Privacy (CA client data, RM KYC data) | Add an on-tool warning: "Don't enter names, clients or employer." Don't store inputs by default. Use an explicit opt-in checkbox for Reel 2 submissions. |
| Students have no job | Add a "target job" mode. |

## Reliability and harm

- **False precision.** A single "% of your job" from an LLM will vary run to run. Viewers will rerun it, get 45% then 62%, and screenshot the mismatch. Fix: use a fixed rubric, temperature 0, and cache per role or task. Show counts ("4 of 11 tasks"), not percentages.
- **Regulated tasks rated "AI does it well."** KYC checks (RBI accountability), GST filing (penalties) and resume screening (bias, legal exposure) are examples. A green or red label here is wrong and embarrassing. Fix: flag regulated tasks as "AI can draft, human must sign off."
- **The teacher problem.** A high number on a shareable card can hurt someone's standing if a principal or manager sees it. Fix: lead the card with "what to hand over and what to grow." Put the bars second, and never show a headline percentage.
- **Misuse by HR or managers** auditing other people. Fix: frame the tool as "your own week," with first-person inputs only.
- **Method attribution.** Naming Anthropic Economic Index and GPTs are GPTs implies your numbers come from them. A critic will check. Fix: say "inspired by task-based research," with a link in the caption only.
- **"AI does it well today"** needs the qualifier "with your checking." It should never read as "this part of you is replaceable."
- **The creator's own number.** If the real result is low, the hook flips (as the screenplay already notes). Decide after the real run, not before.

## Top 5 fixes (highest impact first)

1. **Bring the non-tech job forward.** Keep the 2-second hook, then go straight to the friend's real CA, bank or HR audit by 0:06. Cut the gym scene, or reduce it to a 2-second line. Collapse S2 and S3 into one sentence: "Not your job. Your tasks." Then show the screen.
2. **Switch to Hinglish delivery and Hinglish captions,** with role examples on screen long enough to read (3 to 4, not 6 flickering).
3. **Rebuild the input as tap-to-tick role templates,** with Hindi/Hinglish and voice input, no login, a privacy warning, and a test of the DM flow plus a pinned-link fallback.
4. **Replace percentages with task counts,** both in the hook ("AI can already do 4 of my 11 weekly tasks") and on the card. Add deterministic scoring and regulated-task flags before launch.
5. **Change the CTA to "Comment AUDIT + your job."** The job titles in comments are relatable social proof for the next viewer, they feed Reel 2 research, and they drive replies such as "same, HR here," which help reach and sends.

**Verdict: revise.** The core is strong: the creator goes first, the fear is broken into tasks, and the reel ends on a 2-week plan. But as written it's a tech-audience reel with non-tech examples bolted on. The tool also needs guardrails before a teacher or bank RM screenshots a number that isn't stable or fair.

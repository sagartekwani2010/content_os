## Word count and runtime

- **Count:** beat 10 is 19 words, not 20, so the placeholder total is 118, not 119.
- **Pace:** 2.6 words per second is brisk, and it conflicts with directions like "unhurried", "held look" and "flat, then hold". At a realistic 2.4 words per second, the filled script is 53–57 s of speech plus about 4 s of holds, so 57–61 s. That is at or over the 58 s limit.
- **Tight scenes:** scene 3 is about 22 words in 8 s with a finger count, and scene 4 is about 13 words in 5 s with a 0.5 s hold. Both are over 2.7 words per second.

## Hooks that work

- **The peel and empty slot:** a physical first frame, and a strong stop.
- **The taped row:** it opens at 0:31 and pays off at 0:42. The loop is short enough to hold.
- **"Still mine. For now.":** a real jolt.
- **The dated card and the empty Run #2 outline:** they give the series a credible reason to come back.
- **The non-IT text at 0:03:** right idea, but it is text only while he talks about himself.

## Does the GPT CTA make people act?

Commenting RUN, yes: it is cheap. Running the GPT, mostly no. The path is comment, open the DM, log in to ChatGPT, list 3–5 tasks, invent dummy inputs, then grade each one. That is 5–10 minutes at 10 pm.

The RM, CA and IB viewers also can't use real work, so the result feels hollow. "Bring your number" asks people to post publicly how replaceable they are, with colleagues watching. Most won't.

## Overclaims, smugness and blame

- **Scene 5, "AI from a year ago":** this is the biggest overclaim risk. The prompts were written on today's model, one person graded both, and a year-old model may no longer be callable. "[OLD_N] → [N]" with an arrow reads as a trend however small the disclaimer is.
- **Scene 8, "that time went to [FREED_WORK]" and "I hand over":** these risk a humblebrag. The phrasing assumes control over your own job design that a TCS or bank viewer doesn't have.
- **Scene 2, "I build AI for a living":** some will hear this as a flex ("easy for you"). Nothing in the script blames laid-off people.
- **Scene 6, [BEFORE_TIME] → [AI_TIME]:** a single timing. Keep "includes my check" on screen and never round it up.

## Top 5 fixes

1. **Scenes 1–2:** choose [TASK] so a non-coder recognises it, for example a weekly status report or meeting notes, not unit tests. Make scene 2 point outward: "I build AI for a living. My week isn't safe, and yours?" Keep the on-screen list.
2. **Scene 4:** cut "Run one, eighteenth November" from the voice and keep it on the card. That saves about 4 words and 1.5 s.
3. **Scene 5:** keep it only if the old model actually ran on identical prompts and the gap is large. Say "an older model", not "a year ago", unless the dates are verified. Otherwise cut the scene, which saves about 4 s and the riskiest claim.
4. **Scene 8:** tie the freed time to the human skill from scene 9 instead of a personal win, for example "That hour went to [skill like FAILED_TASK]." It turns a flex into a lesson and sets up the reveal.
5. **Scene 10:** change "Bring your number" to "Bring the task it failed." That is safe to post and a point of pride. Cap the GPT at 3 tasks and about 2 minutes, and put "free · 2 min · use a made-up example" on screen.

## Verdict

**Revise.** The structure, the props and the open loop are strong. Fix the runtime, scene 5, the scene 8 tone and the CTA's posting fear before approving the beat sheet. Re-time the script once the real values are in.

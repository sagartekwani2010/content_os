## 2. Three failure patterns in the weakest hooks

1. **Stance before substance** (#10, 11, 12, 14). These announce a feeling or attitude with no task or number, so the viewer has nothing to picture. The meeting invite and the duck are layoff openers in disguise.
2. **Process before payoff** (#6, 8, 9, 27, 30). Dates, cadence and method are reasons to follow, not reasons to stop. They belong at seconds 5-10 and in the closing line.
3. **About me, not you** (#13, 29, 3). The engineer's identity, gym or screen leads with no doorway for an accountant or teacher. #13 also drifts into doom with "not safe".

## 5. The 10 seconds after #1

Yes, the hook changes them. A question about the viewer creates two debts: how you got your number, and how they get theirs. Credentials and layoffs wait, and the method comes before any story.

| Time | Spoken | Visual |
|---|---|---|
| 3-5 s | "[N] of [TOTAL]. Run one, eighteenth November." | The card goes onto the whiteboard above the task list, with the date written on it. |
| 5-8 s | "Every task I do in a week. Three runs each. Pass, pass with edits, or fail." | Fast pan down the list, with three tick boxes per row and the rubric sheet taped beside it. |
| 8-11 s | "And I build AI for a living. This one, it does better than me." | Your finger on the green row [TASK], then a one-second flash of the real output. |
| 11-13 s | "This one, it failed all three times. Keep that in mind." | Your finger on a red row, with the task name covered by tape. |

The taped row is the open loop that holds viewers to the end. The back-test ([OLD_N]) comes next at about 13 s, and "comment your 3 tasks" pays off the hook's question at the close.
